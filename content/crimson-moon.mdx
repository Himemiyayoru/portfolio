## Why it exists

Werewolf asks for the one thing a socially anxious player can least afford to spend: a room, a turn to speak, and the chance of being the person everyone has decided to suspect. The game is famous because that pressure works. It is also why some people never sit down.

Crimson Moon keeps the structure and removes the crowd. The other seats are held by AI maidens. You still have to talk, answer, and risk a vote. You do not have to do it in front of strangers. Played that way, a match is also practice. The sentence still has to be made, and the table will still wait to hear it.

## Two clocks

One clock is ordinary time. It does not pause, and it is meant to feel sharp. Hesitation has a cost.

The other clock was rebuilt for this player. It moves by exchanges, not by seconds. Until you press next, the table stays where it is. You can assemble a sentence. The room does not punish you for the time that takes.

If composing a speech is the part you would rather skip, preset lines are already in the game. A match can run on a local language model or a cloud one. The cloud model is there for when the lines should come back polished.

## Cards, and no plain villagers

A classic game already assigns a role by a draw. That draw is close enough to a tarot that the identity here is a card.

Upright cards keep the village. Reversed cards are the witches. There is no plain villager waiting out the night with nothing to do. Each seat receives a Major Arcana, and with it an identity and an ability.

The table itself is not fixed. Each match chooses, at random, which maidens sit down and which tarot ability each of them draws. The same cast does not return with the same powers. That is what makes another evening worth playing.

## The village

The story is set in an astrology village. The maidens who live there are untroubled. They divine, they resonate with the moon, and each of them has learned a Major Arcana of her own.

Then the bright moon turns scarlet. Some of the maidens are still resonating when it happens, and the evil in that moon gets into them. Their cards reverse. They become witches. Each night a witch stays alive by taking another maiden's tarot power. The maiden who loses that power dies in pain.

So the village holds a trial every evening. Everyone votes. The person they suspect is executed. The witches keep their faces hidden, and they enjoy the confusion.

Fifteen maidens live in the village. Each one has a history of her own.

## The day

Morning. The maidens wake. Some of them may spend a tarot ability and learn something the others do not have.

Noon. The sun meets the crimson moon, and an anomaly appears. It is drawn from the Minor Arcana. At its worst, it changes the state of the match.

Afternoon tea. The players still alive are split into small rooms. A maiden cannot hear a room she is not in. This is the hour for trading what you know, and for deciding what you will not say when the whole table is listening.

Dusk. Everyone is gathered again. They argue, then vote for the person to execute.

Night. The witches move. They remove the maiden who threatens them most.

## How it was made

The maidens and the tarot cards were generated in Midjourney, then refined in Photoshop. A card, when it is shown, plays an animation made in Runway. The score was generated in Suno. The lyrics are mine. Ordinary play uses the instrumental. At the climax the full song comes in, voice included. The game itself was built in Cursor.
