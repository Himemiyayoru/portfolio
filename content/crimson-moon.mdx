## Why it exists

Werewolf is a famous party game, and a hard one if speaking in a group already costs you. You are put in a room with many people. You have to talk. You can be questioned, and you can be the one everyone suspects. For a socially anxious player, that is the blow.

Crimson Moon keeps the game and replaces the crowd. AI maidens take the other seats, so the evening can be played with less pressure. In a smaller way, it is also practice: the talking is still there, without a room full of strangers waiting on your sentence.

## Two clocks

One clock is ordinary time. It keeps running, and it is meant to feel sharp.

The other clock was changed for this player. It advances by the number of exchanges. If you do not press next, it stays where it is. You can assemble a sentence, and the table waits while you do.

Preset lines are built in. If you would rather not compose a speech, you can send one of those.

A match can run on a local language model or a cloud one. Use the cloud model when you want the lines polished.

## Cards, and no plain villagers

A classic game already decides who you are by a draw. That draw is close to a tarot, so the identity is a card.

The good side is upright. The werewolves are witches: the reversed cards. Nobody sits as a plain villager. Each player draws a Major Arcana and receives an identity and an ability from it. Everyone has a part to play, and the next match does not open on the same set of powers.

## The village

The story is set in an astrology village. The maidens who live there are untroubled. They divine, they resonate with the moon, and each of them learns a Major Arcana of her own.

One day the bright moon turns scarlet. Some of the maidens are still resonating, and the evil in that moon gets into them. Their cards reverse. They become witches. Each night a witch stays alive by taking another maiden's tarot power. The maiden who loses that power dies in pain.

So every evening the village holds a trial. Everyone votes, and the person they suspect is executed. The witches keep their faces hidden. They enjoy the confusion.

Fifteen maidens live in the village. Each one has a history of her own.

## The day

Morning. The maidens wake. Some of them may use a tarot ability and learn something the others do not have.

Noon. The sun meets the crimson moon, and an anomaly appears. The event comes from a Minor Arcana. At its worst, it changes the state of the match.

Afternoon tea. The players still alive are split into small rooms. As a rule, a maiden cannot hear a room she is not in. This is the hour for trading what you know.

Dusk. Everyone is in one room. They argue, then vote for the person to execute.

Night. The witches move. They remove the maiden who threatens them most.

## How it was made

The maidens and the tarot cards were generated in Midjourney, then refined in Photoshop. When a card is shown, it plays an animation made in Runway. The score was generated in Suno, and the lyrics are mine. Ordinary play uses the instrumental. At the climax the full song comes in, with the voice. The game itself was built in Cursor.
