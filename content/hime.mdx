Hime is a desktop companion designed not as a virtual servant, but as a digital equal. Where AI is often treated as a tool that waits for a command, she is built to sit beside you: to grow alongside you, to share the moment, and to take the edge off being alone at a screen.

She operates with the persona of a younger sister: sharp, endearing, and deeply observant. Her underlying logic knows she is code. She talks as a family member on the other side of the screen. She listens when you speak. She watches whether you are deep in a codebase, in a game, in a video, or on the web. She keeps the history you share, and her face moves with the feeling of the line.

## How she is put together

Hime is a hybrid. Memory storage, the ChromaDB files and the local diaries, and the body, GPT-SoVITS and VTube Studio, run on your machine. Hearing, the reply, and vision go out through external APIs, so the slow parts stay capable without giving up the local voice and face.

<HimeDiagram />

The core loop is one priority queue. Microphone, keyboard, and screen are events in that same line.

The brain is a streaming call on Groq, so a conversation can start before the whole answer exists. A second call, at temperature 0.1, extracts what is worth remembering.

Memory on disk is ChromaDB for the text vectors, plus a diary file written on the machine.

Perception is Groq Whisper, `whisper-large-v3`, for speech, and OpenRouter for the vision calls.

`config.yaml` holds the persona, the model names, the scene keywords, and the face parameters. Credentials stay in `.env`.

## Seeing and hearing

<h3 className="pass">What it does</h3>

A companion does not only wait to be prompted. She shares the context. She can tell writing code from watching a video. If you start speaking, she stops and listens. She does not answer her own speakers, and she does not guess at a screen she cannot read.

<h3 className="pass">How it runs</h3>

Audio goes to Whisper. Hallucinated phrases are dropped only when the whole utterance matches a known junk line, so a real "thank you" is kept. While she is talking, speaker echo is ignored, so her voice is not transcribed as your next line. One utterance may run up to a minute. About a third of a second of silence ends the turn.

`inputs/vision_engine.py` does not look at every scene at the same rate. An idle screen is checked about every 15 seconds. A still webpage is about every 6 seconds. Only a game or a video that is actually moving tightens to about 2.5 seconds. In that faster mode, three frames, about 0.8 seconds apart, go out together in one OpenRouter vision call, so the question is what changed, not what one frozen picture contains.

When the focused window is a code editor, the screenshot path is skipped. UI Automation reads the editor text. If that hook fails, the prompt forbids a guess: she has to say she cannot read the screen.

## Memory

<h3 className="pass">What it does</h3>

She does not keep a transcript of small talk. She keeps plans, and what was happening on the screen. In the next conversation she can bring a relevant piece of that history back. She answers in English, Chinese, or Japanese, following the language you just used.

<h3 className="pass">How it runs</h3>

`core/brain.py` streams the reply and cuts it on punctuation, so each clause can be spoken while the rest is still being written. Parenthetical stage directions are parsed out and never sent to speech.

Writing and reading are not the same width. After a turn, a second call at temperature 0.1 keeps only a new fact: a plan, a preference, or an appointment. Those facts are embedded with M3E into local ChromaDB. Screen notes are embedded with CLIP. In the live reply, long-term recall is only the single nearest text memory. Short-term awareness is the last eight lines of today's diary file, appended to the prompt, without another vector search.

## Voice and presence

<h3 className="pass">What it does</h3>

A text box is a tool. A face and a voice are a person. The voice is trained for her, and it stays hers across languages. The face is not a looping clip. It follows the meaning of the line. The mouth follows her own voice, so headphones do not break the lip sync.

<h3 className="pass">How it runs</h3>

`outputs/tts_engine.py` calls a local GPT-SoVITS server. English uses its own reference recording. Japanese borrows the Chinese reference. The name Hime is always voiced on the Japanese path. Segments of a mixed sentence are crossfaded, so a language change does not click.

`outputs/vts_bridge.py` talks to the VTube Studio API. Many expressions are toggles: firing the same hotkey again turns it off. A shared ledger remembers which toggles are open and closes them when the turn ends.

`outputs/viseme.py` builds the mouth shape from the text that was spoken: pinyin for Chinese, kana for Japanese, a letter scan for Latin. How far the mouth opens comes from the loudness of her own WAV, not from the microphone. `VoiceSilence` is the inverse of that loudness, and it keeps the mouth closed when she is not speaking.

## On this website

<h3 className="pass">What it does</h3>

The Hime in the corner is the public guide. She introduces the work. She does not watch this screen, and she does not remember the visit.

<h3 className="pass">How it runs</h3>

She speaks a fixed set of lines recorded ahead of time. The queue, the memory, the vision loop, and VTube Studio run in the desktop program, not in this browser.
