Each feature below is written twice. **What it does** is the piece you can hold. **How it runs** is the system behind it. The phone is React Native on Expo. The service is FastAPI, at `bobs-special-blend.onrender.com`. I made the phone, the physics drawing, and the service with Gemini.

<h3 className="pass">What it does</h3>

This is the screen a cocktail app is expected to open on. Drinks are grouped into families, and each family scrolls sideways: highballs, sours, and the rest. A card shows the drink and opens the recipe. Tap the heart and it is saved. Saved is that same list, kept for later.

<h3 className="pass">How it runs</h3>

The catalog is more than four hundred recipes, seeded from TheCocktailDB and served by `GET /api/v2/recipes`. Each entry keeps a real name, a method, a glass, and components in milliliters. The card photograph is fetched live from TheCocktailDB by that name. A heart writes the recipe id into AsyncStorage on the phone, under `@cocktail_favorites`. The Saved tab filters the catalog down to those ids. Nothing about a heart is sent to the service.
</Split>

<h3 className="pass">What it does</h3>

The cabinet is a kitchen, not a backbar. You mark the bottles and mixers already in the house. The app then tells you what you can make right now, and what you could make if you bought one or two more things. The missing names are written on the card.

<h3 className="pass">How it runs</h3>

Marks stay on the phone in AsyncStorage, under `@cocktail_inventory`. Matching is a request to `POST /api/v2/match`. The service turns the shelf into a set and, for every recipe, subtracts that set from the recipe's ingredients. Zero missing is a perfect match. One or two missing is almost there, with the missing names attached. Closer drinks are sorted first. The ingredient list itself comes from `GET /api/v2/ingredients`, grouped by category.
</Split>

<h3 className="pass">What it does</h3>

You type what you remember of a name. If a recipe contains those letters, it shows up. If you misspell it, the closest real names still appear. The search does not leave the phone.

<h3 className="pass">How it runs</h3>

The catalog is already on the device. Each keystroke filters it in memory. A name that contains the query is an exact hit and stops the search. If nothing contains it, the phone computes Levenshtein distance against every recipe name, keeps distances of three or less, sorts them, and shows the five closest. No search request is sent.
</Split>

<h3 className="pass">What it does</h3>

Ask Bob is the part of drinking alone that is still a conversation. You say how the day went, by typing or by holding the button and talking. He answers the mood before he names a drink. The name is one cocktail from the menu. He does not invent one. The reply opens the real recipe, so the conversation ends on a glass you can make.

<h3 className="pass">How it runs</h3>

Speech is recorded on the phone and posted to `POST /api/v2/transcribe`. The service sends that file to OpenAI Whisper (`whisper-1`) and returns the text. Chat goes to `POST /api/v2/chat_with_bob`. Bob is Llama 3.1 8B Instant on Groq, with the full catalog names in the system prompt, temperature 0.5, a short token cap, and a required JSON shape: a comment, and exactly one menu name or an empty string. The service matches that name exactly, then by partial overlap. A name that is not on the menu is dropped, and Bob is left as conversation. Both routes are rate-limited per address.
</Split>

<h3 className="pass">What it does</h3>

A drink opens on its photograph. Move a measure, swap a bottle, or change the glass, and the photograph gives way to a drawing, because the photograph is no longer the drink you are making. The liquid fits the glass. Past the rim, it stops. You can see it mixed into one color, or poured in layers. Alcohol, sweetness, and volume sit next to the original, with the direction of the change. A substitute is another ingredient in the same family, ranked by how close it is.

<h3 className="pass">How it runs</h3>

The drawing is computed on the phone, in the recipe screen, so a slider does not wait on the network. Seven glasses are modeled as SVG profiles with a capacity: highball, rocks, martini, coupe, flute, Nick and Nora, and shot. Fill is clipped to that capacity. Alcohol and Brix are volume-weighted averages of the ingredients in the glass. Color uses a Beer-Lambert approximation: each channel's absorbance is summed by volume, then converted back to RGB, so a dark bottle darkens the result instead of being averaged away. Layered mode estimates specific gravity as `1 + brix × 0.004 − abv × 0.001` and draws the denser liquid underneath. Mixed mode uses the one blended color. Substitutes come from `GET /api/v2/substitute`. The service scores other ingredients in the same category by the gap in ABV, Brix, pH, and RGB distance, and returns the closest. Each ingredient's baseline, alcohol, Brix, acidity, color, and opacity, was estimated when the catalog was seeded.
</Split>

## Why it exists

<h3 className="pass">What it does</h3>

The project started with a colleague who had just taken the job. Most of what he told were stories about drinking too much, and he had studied mathematics. A recipe is a set of quantities. Change one, and the finished drink should change in a way you can see: color, alcohol, sweetness, and whether the liquids blend or sit in layers.

<h3 className="pass">How it runs</h3>

That calculation is the app. The catalog stays a catalog. The recipe screen is the editor. The glass follows the numbers. I made the phone, the physics drawing, and the service with Gemini. The video above is the piece you can hold.
