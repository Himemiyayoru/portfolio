## Why it exists

The project started with a colleague who had just taken the job. Most of what he told were stories about drinking too much, and he had studied mathematics. The useful connection was between those two facts. A recipe is a set of quantities. Change one, and the finished drink should change in a way that can be calculated: color, alcohol, sweetness, and whether the liquids blend or sit in layers. That calculation is the app.

## Who it is for

It is for someone mixing at home. The cabinet is for a partial shelf, the bottles already in the house. The recipe screen is for a person who wants to change a drink and see the glass before pouring it. Ask Bob is for the evening when the company is the point: you say how the day went, and you still leave with one real drink from the menu.

## What sets it apart

Cocktail apps are catalogs. This one keeps the catalog, then lets the recipe be edited, and draws the edit. Volume, color, alcohol, and sweetness are recomputed from whatever is in the glass. A layered drink shows the change as bands, ordered by density, so a shot makes the difference obvious. A substitute is another ingredient in the same family, ranked by how close it sits in alcohol, sweetness, acidity, and color. Bob can miss the way a person misses. The glass follows the numbers.

## How it is put together

The phone is React Native on Expo, in five tabs: Library, My Cabinet, Search, Ask Bob, and Saved. The service behind it is FastAPI. Recipes began as TheCocktailDB entries and were normalized into milliliters, a method, a glass, and a physics baseline for each ingredient: alcohol by volume, Brix, acidity, color, and how opaque it is. The photographs on the cards still come from TheCocktailDB. The glass is calculated on the phone, so moving a measure does not wait on the network.

Bob and the transcription run on the service. The model is Llama 3.1, called through Groq, and the reply is kept short and in a fixed shape: what he says, and the drink's name. An exact match is tried first, then a partial one. A recommendation that is not on the menu is dropped, and Bob is left as conversation. Speech is transcribed with Whisper and sent on as the message.
